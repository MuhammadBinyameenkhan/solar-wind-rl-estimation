Pulling it together. RQ1 and RQ2 are answered: unconstrained gains are often undeliverable, the time-separated rule with joint scaling is preferable, and both agents learn within the constraint, TD3 better than DDPG. RQ3 is partly answered: in the converter-only system learning saves about a fifth of the reserved headroom, but in the hybrid system there is at most 1.3 percent to gain, and the agents do not beat a tuned VSG. The remaining work is to test plants where the oracle predicts a larger margin. RQ4 is partly answered: the circuit model confirms trajectories and the nadir ordering but halves the TD3 advantage, so the EMT check needs more scenarios and statistics. For the main question: feasibility can be guaranteed cheaply; the value of learning depends on the plant.
