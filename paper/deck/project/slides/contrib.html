5 · Contributions and limitations

What is new, and what is not yet shown

Contributions

Feasibility layer — direction-aware, time-separated headroom requirement, and a comparison of derating rules

Oracle bound — a training-free test of how much adaptive scheduling can gain

Fair protocol — tuned baselines, several seeds, paired tests; residual agents that start as the tuned VSG

Validation chain — MATLAB re-implementation and Simscape EMT; PLL RoCoF spikes and limiter activity quantified

Limitations

Single bus; regional EGAT demand scaled to the microgrid; one site and one year

EMT shares the averaged model’s parameters; 12 scenarios, one policy

Constraint guarantees commitments, not freedom from saturation

Agents trained with clipping, not joint scaling; no hardware test yet

M. B. Khan · Feasible synthetic inertia · Proposal defence

30

Four contributions. First, a feasibility layer that checks virtual inertia and damping against the headroom that remains after dispatch, in the direction of the event and at the instant each mechanism peaks, with a comparison of how to derate. Second, the oracle bound, a cheap go/no-go test before training. Third, an evaluation protocol that makes learning claims trustworthy. Fourth, a validation chain beyond the averaged model. And the limitations, stated plainly: single bus, a regional demand profile scaled to the microgrid, one site; the EMT model shares the averaged model's parameters and covers only twelve scenarios; the constraint guarantees commitments, not freedom from saturation; the agents were trained with clipping; and there is no hardware test yet. Each limitation maps to a task in the plan.
