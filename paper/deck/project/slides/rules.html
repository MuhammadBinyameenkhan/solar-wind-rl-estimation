Research question one. I applied each feasibility rule to five fixed settings on the 200 test scenarios, with the measured load. Without any constraint, the high setting of Study 1, four seconds and thirty per-unit, needs more headroom than is available for 1.9 seconds of every 30-second episode, and the largest setting for 19.6 seconds: in a system with physical inertia, literature-style gains are frequently undeliverable. Scaling both parameters never gives more saturation than clipping, and gives significantly less for the settings the constraint cuts most often, the high and the largest; for the tuned setting, which is rarely cut, the difference is not significant. Scaling also gives a higher return whenever the constraint cuts. The summed rule of my earlier work is overly conservative: with the wide limits of Study 1 it reduces every setting to about two seconds and 11 to 14 per-unit, so higher declared gains no longer change the response.
