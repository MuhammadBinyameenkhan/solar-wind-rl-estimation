Q: What does the PV de-loading cost, and why use it?

How it works

PV runs at Ppv,0 = (1 − d) PMPP with d = 0.15. The reserve d·PMPP can be released in about 80 ms. The agent sets α, the PV share of the fast VSG power; the BESS supplies the rest.

Why

It adds upward headroom exactly when the BESS is discharging for the EMS, and it spares battery throughput (wbess in the cost).

≈ 180 MWh/yr

PV energy forgone if de-loaded all year: 0.8 MW × 8760 h × 17.3 % CF × 15 %

Open question, not yet quantified

The trade-off between curtailed PV energy and extra headroom (e.g. de-load only when hup is low) has not been studied; it can be part of the sensitivity study.

B13

De-loading is an assumption of the Study 2 plant: the PV runs 15 percent below its maximum power point and holds that 15 percent as a fast reserve, released in about 80 ms. It helps exactly in the evening and in heavy-discharge hours when the battery has little upward room. The cost is energy: if de-loaded all year, 0.8 MW times 8760 hours times a 17.3 percent capacity factor times 15 percent is about 180 MWh per year. That figure is my calculation from the plant data, not a simulation output. I have not optimised the de-loading fraction; scheduling it only when headroom is low is an obvious extension and fits in the sensitivity study. Source: Sections 3.3, 3.5.2, Table 4.2.
