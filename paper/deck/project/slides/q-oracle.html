Q: How is the oracle computed, and what does 1.3 % mean?

  • Grid of fixed settings k simulated on N = 40 validation scenarios
  • Best single setting (3 s, 15 pu): return −94.71
  • Best setting chosen per scenario: −93.48
  • Gain at most 1.2 points = 1.3 %; cost: a parameter sweep, no training

Why it is small here

The two-machine coupling gives the VSG ≈ 71 % of each step whatever Jv, Dv are, and secondary control restores f in 10–20 s. The oracle's damping hardly depends on headroom (about 12 pu).

What it bounds, and what not

It bounds scenario-level choice of settings. A schedule that varies within an event is not strictly bounded; the trained agents, which can, also did not beat the tuned VSG.

The oracle compares two things on validation data: the best single fixed setting, and a clairvoyant that picks the best fixed setting separately for every scenario. The difference is the most that any scenario-level adaptive scheduler can gain, and it costs only a parameter sweep. With the measured EGAT load it is 1.2 points of return, 1.3 percent. It is small because the network coupling gives the VSG about 71 percent of each step regardless of its parameters and secondary control acts within 10 to 20 seconds. A fair question is whether within-event adaptation could exceed it; strictly the oracle does not bound that, but the trained agents, which can vary J and D within events, did not exceed the tuned VSG either.
