1 · Research questions

One main question, four research questions

RQ1

Feasibility

How much headroom do J and D need, and how should a controller be limited: summed or time-separated requirement, clipping or joint scaling?

RQ2

Learning

Can DDPG and TD3 schedule J and D in real time within this constraint, in a converter-only and in a hybrid diesel microgrid?

RQ3

Benefit

Compared fairly, does learning beat a tuned VSG, and how much can any adaptive scheduler gain at most?

RQ4

Fidelity

Do the averaged-model conclusions hold in an independent implementation and a three-phase EMT model?

RQ1–RQ3 mirror the three parts of the main question; RQ4 checks that their answers are not artefacts of the averaged model.

The main question splits into four research questions. RQ1 is about feasibility: how much headroom inertia and damping need, and how to limit them. RQ2 asks whether DDPG and TD3 can learn to schedule them within that limit. RQ3 asks whether learning actually helps compared with tuned alternatives, and bounds the maximum possible gain. RQ4 checks that the answers survive outside the averaged model. Each question maps to one objective and to a set of preliminary results that I will show later.
