Q: Where does hreq = 0.08 J + 0.01 D come from?

Study 1, Eq. (3.17), worst case of the grid code: both limits at once

B8

Substitute the grid-code limits into the VSG power law. With RoCoF at 2 hertz per second, the inertial term 2J times RoCoF over f0 is 2J times 0.04, so 0.08 J. With a 0.5 hertz deviation the damping term is D times 0.01. Hence one second of inertia costs as much as eight per-unit of damping, which is why headroom-limited control turns out to be mainly a damping problem. The literature setting of 15 seconds and 60 per-unit needs 1.2 plus 0.6, 1.8 per-unit, 4500 kVA on a 2500 kVA converter, so it is derated in every step. Source: Sections 3.6, 4.2.3. LaTeX (3.17): h_{\mathrm{req}} = 2J\,\frac{\mathrm{RoCoF}_{\max}}{f_0} + D\,\frac{\Delta f_{\max}}{f_0} = 0.08J + 0.01D
