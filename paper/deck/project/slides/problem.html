Three problems in how adaptive VSGs are designed today

Feasibility

J and D are bounded by fixed ranges, never by the power the converter has left after dispatch.

A promise of inertia that cannot be delivered saturates the converter exactly when it is needed.

Evaluation

Learned VSGs are compared with untuned baselines, from one training run.

DRL results vary with seed and checkpoint (Henderson 2018; Agarwal 2021); the true benefit stays unknown.

Fidelity

Most studies stop at averaged (RMS) frequency models.

Will the conclusions survive a three-phase circuit with a PLL and a current-limited converter?

This thesis treats adaptive VSG control as a feasibility problem first and a learning problem second.

M. B. Khan · Feasible synthetic inertia · Proposal defence

5

Three problems. First, feasibility: adaptive schemes limit J and D to fixed ranges, but never check them against the power the converter still has after the energy management system has dispatched it. Second, evaluation: learned controllers are usually compared against hand-picked fixed gains from a single training run, which can overstate the benefit. Third, fidelity: almost all studies use averaged models. My thesis puts feasibility first, then asks whether learning helps.
