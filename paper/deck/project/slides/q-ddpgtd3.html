Q: Why DDPG and TD3, and not SAC or PPO?

  • Continuous actions and a deterministic controller for deployment: both fit
  • They are the methods most used in the learning-VSG literature (Table 2.3), so results are comparable
  • TD3 isolates one known flaw: DDPG over-estimates. Study 1 confirms it on every seed (panel c): DDPG critic above the Monte-Carlo return, TD3 below
  • Identical networks, hyper-parameters, exploration and episode seeds within each study
  • Criollo et al. (2026) already compare SAC; with an oracle gain of 1.3 %, a third algorithm cannot add much in Study 2

B16

Both algorithms give a deterministic continuous policy, which is what a converter controller needs, and they are the standard in the learning-VSG literature, so my results connect to it. The comparison is controlled: networks, hyperparameters, exploration and episode seeds are identical, so the difference is the algorithm. TD3's twin critics address DDPG's overestimation, and Study 1 confirms it on every seed: the DDPG critic overestimates the Monte-Carlo return, the TD3 critic slightly underestimates it. SAC or PPO could be added, but in Study 2 the oracle bound limits any scheduler to 1.3 percent, so the choice of algorithm is not where the answer lies. Source: Sections 3.8, 4.2.2.
